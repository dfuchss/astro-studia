Writing philosophy in Latin in the 40s BC meant inventing most of the words for
it first. Greek had `ποιότης`; Latin had nothing, so _qualitas_. The same is
true of _essentia_, _moralis_, _individuum_, _evidentia_, _infinitas_ — and of
_humanitas_ in the sense everyone now uses.

## Why it mattered

The claim in _De Finibus_ is that the language is not the obstacle people say it
is: Latin is not poorer than Greek, only less exercised. The proof offered is
the book itself.

Two thousand years later the wordlist is load-bearing in every European
language, mostly unnoticed, which is roughly the best outcome a translation
project can have.
