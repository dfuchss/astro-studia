Notae Tironianae

A shorthand system devised to keep up with dictation and senate debate. Some four thousand signs at first, and still in use as a scribal hand a thousand years later.

Built by Tiro, who as secretary had the problem before anyone else did: speech is faster than writing, and a transcript taken after the fact is a paraphrase.

The system is not an abbreviation scheme bolted onto the alphabet. It is a separate set of signs, with the common words given the shortest ones — the same principle every compression scheme has arrived at since.

Its afterlife is the surprising part. Medieval scribes kept it for marginalia and formulae long after the political use had gone, and the & on your keyboard is one of its survivors.
