A letter and a file format

May 30, 2026·correspondence

Nine hundred letters, and what survives of a correspondence depends on who kept the copies.

A correspondence survives because somebody filed it. Not because it was good.

The letters to Atticus survive as a collection because Atticus kept them and someone later thought the archive worth copying. The other half of that conversation — everything Atticus wrote back — is gone entirely. We have a transcript with one speaker removed.

This is the ordinary condition of ancient evidence and it is worth saying out loud, because the shape of what survives is so easy to mistake for the shape of what happened. The letters look like a man writing into a void. He was not.

Nothing is so unbelievable that oratory cannot make it acceptable.

The same problem turns up wherever an archive is one-sided: mailing lists with missing threads, issue trackers where the decisions happened elsewhere. The record is not wrong. It is just not symmetric, and reading it as if it were is how you end up confident about something nobody said.
